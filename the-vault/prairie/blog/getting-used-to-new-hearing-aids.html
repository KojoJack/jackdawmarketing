Getting Used to New Hearing Aids: Your First Two Weeks

By Paige McCombsMay 18, 2026

If you have just been fitted with hearing aids, the first thing to know is this: the early days are an adjustment, and that is completely normal. Your brain has likely been missing certain sounds for years, and suddenly they are back. Giving yourself a little time makes all the difference.

Why everything sounds loud at first

When your hearing changes gradually, your brain quietly turns down the volume on sounds it stops receiving clearly. Hearing aids bring those sounds back, and at first your brain notices every one of them: the kettle, your own footsteps, the rustle of a newspaper. This settles as your brain relearns what to pay attention to and what to tune out.

Your first two weeks, gently

  • Start at home. Wear them in quiet, familiar surroundings first, then build up to busier places.
  • Wear them daily. Consistency helps your brain adapt far faster than wearing them now and then.
  • Read aloud. Reading to yourself for a few minutes a day helps you get used to the sound of your own voice.
  • Build up the time. A few hours a day at first is fine. Increase it as they start to feel natural.
  • Keep notes. Jot down any situations that feel tricky so we can fine-tune at your follow-up.

Small adjustments make a big difference

This is exactly why a fitting is not a one-and-done appointment. As you wear your hearing aids through real conversations and everyday life, we fine-tune the settings so everything sounds as it should. Most people are comfortable within a couple of weeks, and you are never on your own with it.

If something does not feel right, do not wait and hope it improves. Get in touch and we will sort it out together.
